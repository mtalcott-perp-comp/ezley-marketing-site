Is Ezley safe?

Wiring real money to someone you've never met is supposed to feel risky. Here are straight answers to the questions you should be asking — including the ones we'd rather you didn't.

The short version

Your money never goes straight to the seller. When you buy, your payment is held in escrow by a licensed, regulated partner — never by Ezley itself — and the seller is only paid after you have the item and the review window passes. If something goes wrong before then, filing a dispute freezes the money while a named person sorts it out.

That's the whole idea. The rest of this page is the detail behind it — and the honest limits of what we can and can't do for you.

If you're buying

The buyer's questions

What if I pay and the seller just disappears?

They can't take your money and vanish, because they never receive your money up front. Your payment is captured into escrow, not handed to the seller. The seller is only paid after you've taken delivery and the review window has passed. If the handover never happens, the funds don't release — you open a dispute and get your money back.

Where does my money actually sit while it's in escrow?

The constant is that Ezley never holds your money itself — a licensed, regulated partner does. Card and ACH payments are held by our payments processor until release. Larger wire transfers — the usual path for high-value purchases — are held by a licensed escrow provider. Either way, the funds are earmarked to your transaction, kept out of Ezley's own accounts, and not released to the seller until the conditions for release are met.

What if the item is broken, missing parts, or not as described?

You confirm receipt only when you're satisfied — and even after the funds release, you still have a window to open a dispute and freeze them: wrong item, missing parts, damage you didn't catch at handover, never delivered. A dispute puts a real, named Ezley operator on the case with documented steps and the right to escalate. The leverage is structural: the seller wants their money, and they only get it once you're made whole.

How do I know the seller is a real person and not a fake account?

Above modest amounts, sellers can't transact until they've cleared government-ID identity verification. Every account carries a verification tier you can see — from email-only up to a fully verified seller with ID, phone, address, and a transaction history. For high-value categories, you're dealing with a real, identified person who has put their legal identity on the line, not an anonymous handle.

What if the equipment is stolen, or still has a loan against it?

Here's the honest part: we verify the seller's identity, not the item's title. We don't run lien or title searches, and we don't pretend to. What escrow gives you is time and leverage — the money isn't gone, so you can verify the title, serial number, or VIN before you confirm receipt, and dispute if it doesn't check out. For titled goods, do your own title and lien check at handover, exactly as you would in any private sale. We'll tell you that up front rather than let you assume we did it for you.

What happens to my money if Ezley itself goes out of business?

Your escrowed money isn't in Ezley's bank account to begin with — it's held by a licensed, regulated payments or escrow partner, earmarked to your transaction and separate from the money Ezley operates on. It isn't ours to spend, so it isn't ours to lose. And Ezley isn't an anonymous brand: it's operated by Tarheel Solutions, LLC, a real, registered North Carolina company you can look up and hold accountable — see who's behind Ezley.

Honestly — is this just a scam site?

Fair question to ask of anyone holding your money. So don't take our word for it: the company, the founder, and how to independently verify both are laid out on our About page — including a link to the North Carolina Secretary of State's record. A scam hides its operators behind a logo. We put a name, a registered company, and a public paper trail in front of yours.

If you're selling

The seller's questions

How do I know the buyer can actually pay?

You don't hand over anything until the money is already captured into escrow. By the time you arrange the handover, the buyer's funds are confirmed and held — you're not trusting a promise to pay, you're seeing payment that's already locked in and waiting to release to you.

Can a buyer pay, take the item, then reverse the charge?

That's exactly why high-value transactions are paid by wire or ACH, not card. Above a certain price we don't offer card payment — not to be difficult, but because a wire can't be charged back weeks later the way a card can. On a $50,000 tractor, no-chargeback isn't a detail; it's the point.

When do I actually get paid?

After the buyer confirms receipt, or after the agreed review window passes following handover — whichever comes first. Escrow then releases to your account and funds settle to your bank. No chasing the buyer, no waiting on a personal check to clear.

What if a buyer files a bogus dispute to get a free item?

A dispute isn't a refund button — it opens a documented process with a named operator, not an automatic reversal. The same audit trail that protects the buyer protects you: every step of the transaction is timestamped and recorded, so a baseless claim has a record to answer to. Either party can escalate, and the steps are the same for everyone.

What Ezley does not do

Trust runs both ways, so here's the part most marketplaces leave off. Escrow and identity verification cover a lot — but not everything, and we'd rather you know the edges than discover them.

  • We don't inspect the item for you. We're not a mechanic or an appraiser. Inspect at handover — that's what the review window is for.

  • We don't run title or lien searches. For titled goods, verify the title, VIN, or serial number yourself before you confirm receipt.

  • We don't guarantee value. We make the transaction safe; we don't promise you negotiated a good price. That part's still on you.

  • We won't ever ask you to pay outside Ezley. Escrow only protects you when the payment runs through Ezley. Anyone asking you to wire them directly, off-platform, is the exact risk Ezley exists to remove — walk away and tell us.
